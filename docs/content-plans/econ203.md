## Course summary

ECON 203 (Concordia, Fall 2026, coordinated across sections; Desjardins and Kim for CC/RM1) is a one-term introduction to macroeconomics built on Ragan, *Macroeconomics*, 18th Canadian edition. It covers Chapters 4–16 and 19 in order: the key macro variables (output gap, unemployment, inflation, interest and exchange rates), national income accounting (GDP from the expenditure and income sides, real vs nominal, the deflator), the Keynesian aggregate-expenditure model with and without government and trade (consumption/saving functions, the simple multiplier $1/(1-z)$ with $z = (1-t)c - m$), the AD/AS model and the adjustment of factor prices that anchors output to $Y^*$, long-run growth (saving–investment, the Neoclassical model), money and fractional-reserve banking (the deposit multiplier $1/v$), bonds, money demand and the monetary transmission mechanism, Bank of Canada monetary policy (overnight-rate targeting, 2% inflation target), inflation and disinflation (validation, the acceleration hypothesis, the sacrifice ratio), unemployment and the NAIRU, government debt and deficits (debt dynamics $\Delta d = x + (r-g)d$), and exchange rates with the balance of payments ($CA + KA = 0$, PPP, flexible vs fixed rates). Assessment is 8 MyLab labs (20%), a multiple-choice midterm on Chapters 4–10 (30%, Nov 8) and a cumulative multiple-choice final on Chapters 4–16 and 19 (50%); the outline stresses algebra, definitions, interpretation and multi-part questions, which is why the deck leans on formulas, directions of effects and confusable pairs.

## Conventions chosen

- **Variable names** follow the slides, including their own inconsistencies: $Y$ actual national income, $Y^*$ potential output, $Y_D$ disposable income, $C, I, G, X$; actual (as opposed to desired) values carry a subscript $a$ ($C_a, I_a, G_a, NX_a$). **Imports** are written $IM$ on the Ch. 4–6 slides and in every figure/table (Fig. 7-1, 7-2, 7-3), but $M$ on the Ch. 7 formula slides ($M = mY$, $NX = X - M$, $AE = C + I + G + X - M$); items cite whichever their slide uses and the import item says so. $T$ net tax revenue, $t$ net tax rate, $BB = T - G$ budget balance, $m$ marginal propensity to import, $c$ the MPC when it appears inside a formula (Ch. 7 and 9 slides), $z$ marginal propensity to spend out of national income, $sm$ the simple multiplier, $A$ autonomous expenditure, $S_{PVT}$ / $S_{GOV}$ / $NS$ private, public and national saving, $F_\theta$ the production function with $\theta$ = technology.
- **Ch. 11**: $v$ target reserve ratio, $c$ the cash-to-deposit ratio, $\Delta DEP = \Delta RES \cdot 1/v$ and $\Delta RES \cdot 1/(c + v)$; money supply = currency + bank deposits; the course's aggregates are **M2** and **M2+** (not M1). Bank balance sheets follow Tables 11-3 to 11-8 (reserves, loans | deposits, capital).
- **Ch. 12**: $PV = R/(1+i)^t$; $M_D = M_D(i, Y, P)$ with signs $(-, +, +)$; vertical $M_S$; investment demand $I^D$; transmission chain as in Figures 12-6 and 12-7.
- **Ch. 13**: the policy instrument is the target for the **overnight interest rate**, announced eight times a year; the **bank rate** is 0.25 percentage points above the target (the Bank lends at the bank rate and pays the target rate on deposits, so the actual overnight rate stays in that band); the money supply is endogenous; expansionary = lower target, contractionary = higher target; inflation target 2% within a 1–3% range; **open-market operations** are purchases/sales of government securities.
- **Ch. 14**: Change in money wages = output-gap effect + expectational effect; Actual inflation = output-gap inflation + expected inflation + supply-shock inflation; **demand inflation** vs **supply inflation**; **monetary validation**; **acceleration hypothesis**; disinflation phases 1 (removing validation), 2 (stagflation), 3 (recovery); sacrifice ratio = cumulative GDP loss (% of $Y^*$) / percentage-point fall in inflation.
- **Ch. 15**: $u^*$ = NAIRU; cyclical unemployment = deviations of real GDP from $Y^*$; frictional + structural = NAIRU; states $E, U, N$; average duration of unemployment $U_S/U_O$; **market-clearing** vs **non-market-clearing** theories (the slides do not use the labels "New Classical"/"New Keynesian"); hysteresis.
- **Ch. 16**: $DEF = \Delta D = G + iD - T$, $PDEF = G - T$, $D = \sum DEF_i$; **budget deficit function**; structural (cyclically adjusted) deficit; debt dynamics $\Delta d = x + (r - g)d$ with $x$ the primary deficit ratio, $r$ the *real* interest rate on government bonds, $g$ real GDP growth; crowding out / crowding in; counter-cyclical fiscal policy.
- **Ch. 19**: $CA + KA = 0$; current account = trade account + capital-service account; capital account includes the **official financing account**; exchange rate $e$ as in Ch. 4 (see **Exchange rate** below; C\$1.35 per US\$1 in March 2024); supply of and demand for *foreign exchange* (not for C\$); $CA = NS - I$; PPP: $P_C = eP_E$, $e^{PPP} = P_C/P_E$.
- **Symbol clashes** the slides leave in place: $c$ is the MPC in Ch. 7 and 9 but the cash-to-deposit ratio in Ch. 11 (`money-creation-cash-drain` flags it); $t$ is the net tax rate in Ch. 7 but the number of years in $PV = R/(1+i)^t$ (Ch. 12); $M$ is imports on the Ch. 7 formula slides but money in $M_D$, $M_S$ and M2; **hysteresis** is the short-run path of GDP affecting $Y^*$ in Ch. 12 (12-22) but recessions raising the NAIRU in Ch. 15 (15-23). The two hysteresis items point at each other; a multiple-choice question could use either sense.
- **Sources**: an item cites the slide its fact comes from. Worked examples with numbers of their own (`money-creation-cash-drain-example`, `debt-stance-dynamics-example`, `inflation-reducing-sacrifice-example`) cite the slide whose formula they apply.
- **GDP identity**: $GDP = C_a + I_a + G_a + NX_a$ exactly as slide 5-10 writes it (Table 5-1 labels net exports $X - IM$); the income side is factor incomes + indirect taxes net of subsidies + depreciation (+ statistical discrepancy).
- **Output gap** $= Y - Y^*$ (negative = recessionary, positive = inflationary).
- **Unemployment rate** $u = U / LF \times 100$ with $LF = E + U$ (Ch. 4 slide 8). The **participation rate** ($LF$ / working-age population aged 15+) is not on any slide; it is written from general knowledge.
- **Price indexes**: GDP deflator $=$ nominal / real $\times 100$ (Ch. 5 slide 17); real GDP $=$ nominal / deflator $\times 100$; inflation $= (P_t - P_{t-1}) / P_{t-1} \times 100$ (general; the slides only define it in words). The deck follows the slides in distinguishing the deflator (prices of Canadian *production*) from the CPI (prices consumers *buy*).
- **Real interest rate** $=$ nominal $-$ inflation (Ch. 4 slide 18, no Fisher cross-term).
- **Exchange rate** is the Ragan/course definition: *Canadian dollars per unit of foreign currency*, so an **appreciation of the C\$ is a fall in the exchange rate**. Every exchange-rate item (Ch. 4, 7, 12, 13, 16, 19) uses this direction; it is the single most error-prone convention in the course.
- **Keynesian model**: $APC = C/Y_D$, $MPC = \Delta C / \Delta Y_D$, $APC + APS = 1$, $MPC + MPS = 1$; equilibrium $AE = Y$; simple multiplier $sm = \Delta Y / \Delta A = 1/(1 - z)$ with $z = c$ in the closed no-government model and $z = (1 - t)c - m$ with government and trade (slides 7-13, 7-16, 7-21, 9-22); $T = tY$, $Y_D = (1 - t)Y$, $BB = T - G$; $NX = X - mY$. Worked numbers are the slides' own: $C = 30 + 0.8Y$, $I = 75$, $AE = 105 + 0.8Y$, $Y_0 = 525$ (Ch. 6); $t = 0.1$ turns $C = 30 + 0.8Y_D$ into $30 + 0.72Y$, and Figure 7-3 has $I = 75$, $G = 51$, $NX = 72 - 0.1Y$, $AE = 228 + 0.62Y$, $Y = 600$; the Canadian calibration on slide 7-16 is $c = 0.80$, $t = 0.25$, $m = 0.35$, so $z = 0.25$ and $sm = 1.3$. The simple multiplier is also the *horizontal* shift of AD (Ch. 8 and Ch. 12 slide 17).
- **AD/AS**: AD slopes down for two reasons in Ch. 8 (real-wealth effect on $C$, relative-price effect on $NX$) plus a third in Ch. 12 (higher $P$ raises $M_D$, raises $i$, lowers $I$); a price change *shifts AE* but *moves along AD*; AD shocks move $P$ and $Y$ together, AS shocks move them oppositely; shocks are named positive/negative by their effect on $Y$.
- **Long-run growth**: $S_{PVT} = Y^* - T - C$, $S_{GOV} = T - G$, $NS = Y^* - C - G$ (slide 10-12); production function $GDP = F_\theta(L, K, H)$ (slide 10-17).
- **Markup**: `\$` for money, `\%` inside math, `\dfrac` for displayed fractions, cloze deletions placed inside `\dfrac{...}{{{c1::...}}}` where the formula itself is the thing to recall.

## Verified against the slide images

The slide PDFs embed their equation font without a usable encoding, so text extraction turns every formula into symbol soup. All 14 chapters were therefore checked page by page against rendered PNGs of the slides.

**Chapters 4–10 (first pass).** Every one of the 122 slide pages cited by the sourced items was compared with its item. All directions of effect, definitions and numbers in the first draft were confirmed; the corrections were notation or newly legible numbers: $GDP = C_a + I_a + G_a + NX_a$ (5-10); $C = 30 + 0.8Y$ and $Y_0 = 525$ (6-18, 6-22); the simple multiplier is named $sm$ (6-24, 7-16, 7-19, 7-21); $BB = T - G$ (7-5); Ch. 7 and 9 formula slides write imports as $M$ and the MPC as $c$, $z = (1 - t)c - m$; slide 7-16's Canadian calibration $c = 0.80$, $t = 0.25$, $m = 0.35 \Rightarrow sm = 1.3$ replaced a guessed example; Figure 7-3's full table ($C = 30 + 0.72Y$, $I = 75$, $G = 51$, $X - IM = 72 - 0.1Y$, $AE = 228 + 0.62Y$, $Y = 600$) became its own item; $S_{PVT}$, $S_{GOV}$, $NS$ (10-12); $GDP = F_\theta(L, K, H)$ (10-17); the exchange-rate item quotes slide 4-20's wording.

**Chapters 11–16 and 19 (second pass).** These units were first written from general knowledge of Ragan (tagged `ahead`). When the slides arrived, every page of the seven decks (197 pages) was read and the three units were rebuilt as seven chapter units whose subtopics follow the slides' section numbering. Each of the 56 former `ahead` items was rewritten to the slides' notation, numbers and claims (recorded in Conventions above) or deleted; only `money-nature-fiat` and `money-banking-fractional` kept their keys. Claims dropped because no slide states them: $MV = PY$ (the slides make the money–inflation link empirically, Figure 12-9), M1 as a course aggregate (11-29 uses M2 and M2+), a fourth Bank of Canada function and the Governor's term (11-10), "no legal reserve minimum", "18–24 months" of policy lag (13-19 gives two reasons instead), "since 1991" and "renewed every five years" for the inflation target, the "New Classical"/"New Keynesian" labels (15-11 to 15-14) and minimum wages as a cause of a higher NAIRU. Invented examples were replaced by the slides' own: deposit creation with $v = 0.2$ (\$100 $	o$ \$500, Tables 11-3 to 11-8), present values \$95.24 and \$1078.73 (12-4, 12-5), and the car-parts vs car-assembly case (15-16).

**Slide typos found** (the deck keeps the correct statement and does not reproduce them): 4-8 \"If $Y > Y^*$, positive cyclical unemployment\" (should be $<$); 4-6 lists the cycle phases in a different order than its own figure; 7-13 \"$AE$ = autonomous expenditure $- zY$\" (should be $+$); 9-8 titled \"Output Above Potential, $Y < Y^*$\" with below-potential bullets; 12-7 \"leads to a decrease (increases)\".

The 8 un-sourced items in slide-covered chapters (`macro-labour-participation-rate`, `macro-inflation-rate-formula`, `macro-inflation-real-rate-direction`, `gdp-expenditure-imports-sign`, `gdp-real-which-grew`, `gdp-real-growth-rate`, `gdp-omissions-gdp-vs-gnp`, `growth-basics-rule-of-70`) are stated on no slide in any of the 14 chapters, so they stay un-sourced (general knowledge that exams routinely test).
